# Arie Klootwijk - SE 301 717 B research package

Prepared for Tom Klootwijk, 14 August 2026.

## Purpose

This package examines what Arie Klootwijk and his co-inventors were actually developing in the patent family beginning with the 15 June 1959 Netherlands priority filing and represented here by Swedish patent SE 301 717 B.

The package was assembled to test a specific hypothesis: whether the patent's `SO2` language and its insistence on a nearly equimolar dihydroxy/diepoxide feed concealed a radically different technology beneath ordinary applications such as fibres, moulding and extrusion.

## Core conclusion

The evidence does **not** support a disguised sulfur-dioxide release, biological-sulfur, ion-exchange or unrelated catalytic technology. In this patent, `SO2` is a neutral diaryl-sulfone bridge, `Ar-S(=O)2-Ar`, covalently built into the monomers and polymer backbone.

The real advance is nevertheless unusually forward-looking. The inventors combined:

- a rigid, polar sulfone bridge for thermal and mechanical performance;
- bifunctional phenol/epoxide ring-opening to make a **linear poly(hydroxy ether)** rather than a cured epoxy network;
- near-equivalent functional-group balance to control step-growth molecular weight and end groups;
- dry, pure reactants and substantially water-free phenolate catalysis;
- polar, nonreactive solvents chosen to prevent premature crystallisation or precipitation; and
- deliberate stopping/end-group control after the desired molecular weight had been reached.

This amounts to an early **reactive high-performance thermoplastic platform**. It can be processed as a thermoplastic while retaining a secondary hydroxyl group at every epoxide-opening event, giving adhesion, hydrogen bonding, blend compatibility and possible later crosslinking or derivatisation.

## Strongest modern application readings

The most evidence-supported application families are:

1. dielectric, adhesive and build-up films for electronics;
2. coatings, primers and hot-melt/reactive adhesives;
3. thermoplastic tougheners and reactive interlayers in epoxy/composite systems;
4. moulded, extruded or fibre products with a higher thermal window than conventional BPA phenoxy;
5. corrosion-resistant and barrier layers; and
6. membrane blends or bio-device interfaces as research hypotheses requiring substantial qualification.

Direct implants, drug delivery, intrinsic ion exchange, proton-exchange membranes and SO2 storage/release are unsupported by the patent record.

## Rebuilding the report

From `00_Review/`, run `./build_report.sh`. It expects Pandoc, XeLaTeX, Lato and DejaVu Sans Mono. The figure source images must remain in `../05_Figures/`.

The figures can be regenerated by running `python 05_Figures/build_figures.py` from the package root, with Python, NumPy, pandas and Matplotlib installed.

## Evidence and interpretation discipline

The package distinguishes:

- statements directly supported by the original patent and its U.S. counterpart;
- later industrial or scientific context;
- polymer-chemistry calculations; and
- new hypotheses that require experiments.

The presence of a later patent in the package shows relevance or technological lineage, not necessarily direct commercial continuity from the 1959 work.

## Safety and regulatory note

The historical chemistry involves epoxides, strong bases, hot polar solvents, inert-gas operation and potentially pressurised apparatus. The proposed validation programme is a research framework, not a turnkey synthesis protocol. Any reproduction requires competent personnel, appropriate containment and a formal hazard review.

Bisphenol S monomer hazard information must not be automatically assigned to a purified high-molecular-weight polymer, but residual BPS, DGE-BPS, low oligomers and degradation products require modern analytical and toxicological evaluation, especially for food-contact, medical, bioengineering or consumer-exposure uses.
