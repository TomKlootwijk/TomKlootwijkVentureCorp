# Modern reproduction and validation program

## Objective

Recreate the three most informative architectures, quantify the relationship between stoichiometry, conversion, topology and properties, and decide whether the BPS poly(hydroxy ether) is most promising as a free thermoplastic, a reactive film/adhesive, a thermoset toughener or a membrane blend component.

This is a research plan, not a turnkey operating procedure. A competent laboratory should perform a formal hazard review for epoxides, strong bases, hot polar solvents, pressure and oxygen exclusion before execution.

## Phase 1 - identity, purity and analytical standards

### Monomers

Characterize BPS and DGE-BPS before polymerization:

- BPS 4,4'/2,4' isomer ratio by HPLC or quantitative NMR;
- water by Karl Fischer titration;
- DSC melting profile and impurity shoulders;
- DGE-BPS epoxy equivalent;
- hydrolyzable and total chlorine;
- alpha-glycol and other ring-opened impurity content;
- LC-MS profile of low-level monofunctional or oligomeric species; and
- metals by ICP-MS when electrical or biological uses are contemplated.

Prepare analytical standards for BPS, DGE-BPS and representative low oligomers so that later extractables work is quantitative rather than merely qualitative.

### Benchmark architecture set

Use a 2 x 2 monomer matrix:

1. BPA + DGEBA: no sulfone control;
2. BPS + DGEBA: sulfone in the diphenol only;
3. BPA + DGE-BPS: sulfone in the diepoxide only; and
4. BPS + DGE-BPS: sulfone in both monomers.

This mirrors the logic of the U.S. comparison and separates sulfone dose from molecular weight.

## Phase 2 - stoichiometry and process-window design

### Effective ratios

Run effective dihydroxy:diepoxy ratios of approximately 0.98, 0.99, 1.00, 1.01 and 1.02, explicitly counting any phenol present as catalyst salt. Include a controlled monofunctional end-capper series.

### Catalyst series

Start with anhydrous monosodium BPS to reproduce the patent. Then compare low-residue alternatives appropriate to the intended application. For electronic use, catalyst-derived Na, N and P must be tracked. For biological use, all catalytic residues and counterions must be included in the extractables assessment.

### Solvent and phase-state series

Reproduce acetophenone and sulfolane conditions, then evaluate a smaller modern solvent set selected by:

- ability to dissolve both monomers and the growing polymer;
- chemical inertness toward epoxide and phenol;
- boiling point and pressure requirements;
- dielectric behavior at reaction temperature;
- ease of polymer isolation and solvent removal; and
- worker and environmental risk.

Measure turbidity or light scattering during reaction. The central patent claim can be tested directly by correlating the onset of phase separation with molecular weight and final toughness.

### In-process monitoring

Use complementary measurements:

- ATR-FTIR or Raman for epoxide conversion;
- reaction calorimetry for heat release and rate changes;
- online or frequent-sample rheology/solution viscosity;
- SEC/GPC with multi-angle light scattering where solubility allows;
- quantitative NMR for end groups at lower molecular weight; and
- mass balance on isolated polymer and soluble oligomer fractions.

## Phase 3 - molecular architecture

For each material determine:

- Mn, Mw and dispersity;
- intrinsic viscosity in the historical DMF method for bridge-to-patent comparability;
- branching index using SEC-viscometry or light-scattering methods;
- residual epoxide and phenolic/secondary OH content;
- soluble gel fraction and evidence of microgel;
- repeat structure by NMR and FTIR;
- crystallinity and polymorph by WAXS/DSC; and
- chain-end identity after deliberate phenol-rich or epoxide-rich feeds.

A decisive experiment is to compare the patent's effective 1.02:1 Example-1 feed with a nominal 0.97:1 feed that ignores catalyst-bound BPS. The two should produce different end-group balances and ideal molecular-weight ceilings.

## Phase 4 - thermal and mechanical qualification

### Thermal

- DSC and modulated DSC for Tg, melting and recrystallization;
- DMA for storage modulus, tan delta and service-temperature window;
- TGA in nitrogen and air;
- isothermal melt rheology at 230, 250 and 290 C;
- molecular-weight retention after controlled residence times;
- heat-distortion and Vicat tests using current methods; and
- physical-aging and annealing studies near 160 C.

### Mechanical

- tensile stress-strain with modulus, yield, break and toughness;
- notched and unnotched impact;
- fracture toughness;
- creep and stress relaxation at elevated temperature;
- fatigue and environmental stress cracking; and
- property retention after water, base, acid and humidity exposure.

A modern study should not treat increasing yield stress after annealing as automatically beneficial. Ductility, impact and fracture behavior must be measured in parallel.

## Phase 5 - application modules

### Electronics and dielectric film

Measure:

- film formation and copper adhesion;
- dielectric constant and loss versus frequency and humidity;
- volume/surface resistivity and breakdown strength;
- thermal expansion and warpage in laminate stacks;
- ionic contamination, especially Na, chloride, N and P residues;
- laser-drill/desmear compatibility; and
- solder-reflow and pressure-cooker aging.

### Coating, adhesive and composite matrix

Evaluate:

- lap shear and peel on aluminium, steel, copper, glass and carbon fibre;
- hot-melt open time and melt viscosity;
- crosslinking with representative blocked isocyanate or other OH-reactive chemistry;
- cure conversion and network density;
- phase behavior when blended into epoxy thermosets; and
- interlaminar fracture toughness in composite coupons.

### Barrier and membrane

For dense films measure OTR, CO2 transmission and WVTR under dry and humid conditions. For phase-inversion or electrospun membranes measure pore size, porosity, tensile strength, flux, protein adsorption, fouling, cleanability and sealability. Blend studies with PES should include phase diagrams and leachable fractions.

### Bioengineering and bioprocess

Before direct biological contact:

- perform exhaustive and simulated-use extractions;
- quantify BPS, DGE-BPS, oligomers and reaction by-products by LC-MS/MS and high-resolution MS;
- quantify residual solvents and metals;
- test chemical and molecular-weight change after intended sterilization cycles;
- conduct risk-based biological evaluation under the applicable ISO 10993 framework; and
- use worst-case surface-area-to-volume and time/temperature conditions.

## Phase 6 - decision logic

A useful modern material should satisfy a role-specific benchmark, not simply reproduce a 1959 recipe.

- **Free thermoplastic:** stable melt window, reproducible molecular weight, toughness and creep performance competitive with an incumbent.
- **Reactive film/adhesive:** high film strength and adhesion, controlled crosslinking, low ionic and low-molecular-weight residue.
- **Thermoset toughener:** predictable miscibility/phase separation and a measurable fracture-toughness gain without unacceptable Tg loss.
- **Membrane blend:** improved toughness or sealability without flux, fouling or leachables penalty.
- **Bio-contact material:** extractables and biological risk acceptable for the exact exposure route; otherwise restrict use to non-contact structural or device layers.

## Most informative first experiment

A compact first campaign would make the four benchmark architectures at matched absolute molecular weight, then compare Tg/Vicat, water uptake, dielectric behavior, impact and adhesion. Matching molecular weight is crucial: it would distinguish the chemical contribution of the sulfone bridge from the molecular-weight contribution that partially confounds the historical Table 1.
