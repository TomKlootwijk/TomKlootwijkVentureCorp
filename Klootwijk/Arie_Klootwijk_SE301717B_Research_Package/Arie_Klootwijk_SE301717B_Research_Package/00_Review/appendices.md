# Conclusions

## Final judgement

Arie Klootwijk's work was not hiding a non-polymer SO$_2$ technology. It was more interesting than that interpretation in a different way: it was an early, coherent design for a **reactive high-performance thermoplastic**.

The sulphur dioxide notation points to a rigid neutral diaryl-sulfone bridge that raises thermal and mechanical performance. The equimolar window is a precise chain-length control in bifunctional polymerisation. The solvent requirement is a way to keep growing chains mobile through the last, molecular-weight-defining increments of conversion. The anhydrous catalyst and purity limits protect effective functionality. The final secondary OH groups give adhesion and a latent route to crosslinking.

The strongest modern reading is therefore:

> **A sulfone-enhanced phenoxy platform designed to be processed as a thermoplastic, with enough built-in hydroxyl chemistry to become an adhesive, reactive film, thermoset modifier or functional interface.**

That is genuinely ahead of the ordinary wording about fibres and mouldings. It does not require a hidden cipher; the revolutionary part is the integration of polymer reaction engineering and latent functionality.

## Priority next step

The most informative experimental programme is not to reproduce only one historical example. It is to make four matched-molecular-weight polymers - BPA/DGEBA, BPS/DGEBA, BPA/DGE-BPS and BPS/DGE-BPS - at controlled effective ratios, then compare:

- absolute molecular weight and branching;
- Tg, Vicat, crystallinity and melt stability;
- impact, fracture toughness and creep;
- water uptake and property retention;
- adhesion and controlled post-crosslinking;
- dielectric performance; and
- BPS/DGE-BPS/oligomer extractables.

That programme would separate the sulfone effect from molecular weight, validate the subtle catalyst/ratio bookkeeping, and reveal whether the most valuable modern form is a free thermoplastic, an adhesive film, a composite toughener or a membrane blend component.

# Carothers calculations

For a linear AA + BB system with `r` defined as the smaller initial functional-group population divided by the larger and `p` as conversion of the limiting groups:

$$X_n = \frac{1+r}{1+r-2rp}$$

At complete limiting-group conversion:

$$X_{n,\max} = \frac{1+r}{1-r}$$

At exact balance, `r = 1`:

$$X_n = \frac{1}{1-p}$$
